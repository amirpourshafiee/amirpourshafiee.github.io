NASA Centennial Challenges, Sample Return Robot 2013

UCSC Autonomous Rover

An autonomous rover built for NASA's Sample Return Robot challenge. It used computer vision to recognize a pre-cached sample and a path-finding algorithm to drive to it. To mimic conditions off Earth, competitors could not use compasses, GPS, or GSM.

The 30 kg rover rides on a rocker-bogie suspension for rough terrain and can turn in place. Its feedback control uses a gyroscope, 2 encoders, 3 panning infrared proximity sensors, force sensing resistors, impact sensors for sample collection, and 3 cameras. We moved navigation off the gyroscope and onto wheel encoders partway through the build after gyro drift proved too noisy to trust outdoors.

D* Lite path planning brought the rover close to the goal, and a Haar cascade classifier found the sample in the field. A finite-state machine sequenced the run: search, approach, collect, and return, with the return leg replanning home with D* Lite and scanning for a QR code left on the starting platform. We tested the full sequence repeatedly on UCSC's OPERS field before the competition.

I co-led the team and designed the sensor subsystems. The rover qualified on May 8 with a clean autonomous run, then competed at WPI on June 4 to 5 against 13 other teams. In the competition run it found the sample but knocked it over on approach due to a camera misalignment, then couldn't make it back to the start after a wheel pivot servo failed. The team still received a NASA Technology Achievement Award for the Sample Return Robot Challenge 2013.

Team: Geoff Budd, Amir Pourshafiee, Sina Kahnemouyi, Samir Mohammed, Tuan Ho, Leonardo Bravo
